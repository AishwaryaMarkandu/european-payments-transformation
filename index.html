Executive summary

Compliance got banks to the starting line. The operating model decides who stays in the race.

My reference bank passed the 2025 deadlines the way most banks did, by wrapping a batch-era stack in an instant gateway. It works. It also leaves almost no room for a bad day, and volume is growing fast.

What I found

Compliant, not transformed

Across my eight lenses the bank scores 2.0 out of 5 on average. Compliance is its strongest lens at 3.0. Architecture and exception handling are its weakest at 1.5.

What I found

The 10 seconds are 91% spent

In my latency model, the bolt-on stack uses 9.1 s at p99. A slow beneficiary bank or a busy fraud engine is enough to miss the target. My redesigned flow needs 2.5 s.

What I found

The problems sit in the seams

Fraud, sanctions, liquidity and exceptions run as separate silos. A Saturday-night incident becomes a Monday-morning problem, and the customer feels every hour of it.

What I recommend

2026  Foundation: make every payment visible, agree the target.
2027  Process transformation: one decision layer, 24/7 operations.
2028  Automation: exceptions and liquidity handled by software first.
2029  Intelligent operations: predict, adapt, learn.

My decision ask

I would ask the executive committee for three things in 2026: approval of the target architecture, approval of a 24/7 operating model, and a phased envelope of about €48M released gate by gate. Nothing in year one is irreversible, which is deliberate.

Why now

The deadlines are behind us. The volume curve is not.

I read the Instant Payments Regulation as a floor. It fixes what a bank must offer. It says nothing about how well the machine behind it runs.

Regulation

Send, receive, verify the payee, screen customers daily, and charge no more than for a standard transfer. Resilience rules under DORA apply on top.

Market

Instant payments grew 73% in 2025 and now make up more than a third of SEPA credit transfers. Every manual workaround scales with that curve.

Threat

An instant payment cannot be pulled back by waiting for the batch. Fraud moves to the channels where money is irrevocable within seconds.

A
My read

The regulation made speed a commodity. What remains scarce is doing it safely, cheaply and at 03:00 on a Sunday.

My design choice

I treat friction as a budget. Almost every payment should feel like nothing. The few that deserve a pause get a message that explains why, because a warning people click through protects nobody.

Lens · Settlement and liquidityToday 2.0 / 52029 target 4.5 / 5

Is there liquidity at 03:00 on a Sunday?

My finding: instant payments settle in central bank money around the clock through TIPS, but a bank tops up its liquidity through T2, which follows business-day opening hours. So the weekend buffer has to be sized on Friday. The bank sizes it on a four-week average, and a single Saturday-night spike breaks it.

Cumulative net outflow (synthetic weekend)Buffer sized on an averageBuffer sized on my P99 forecast
AS-IS
  • Static pre-funding, adjusted by hand on business days
  • Liquidity tracked in spreadsheets, no event calendar
  • Treasury reachable Monday, not at 22:00 Saturday
TO-BE
  • Friday pre-funding sized on a P99 forecast plus an event calendar
  • Alerts at 70% usage, dynamic limits on low-priority high-value flows
  • One liquidity cockpit shared by Treasury and Payments Ops

Curve is synthetic and normalised to 100 units. I would check the current T2 and TIPS operating calendar before sizing a real buffer.

Lens · Exception managementToday 1.5 / 52029 target 4.5 / 5

When the happy path breaks, a person opens a spreadsheet.

My finding: 680 of every 10,000 instant payments need a human today. With an exception taxonomy and automated triage I take that to 60. The biggest wins are in the categories nobody owns, such as timeouts and invalid data.

Met by effort today, to be met by design tomorrow.

My finding: this is the bank's best lens, and it still depends on people compiling evidence. I moved each obligation from "someone proves it" to "the system proves it".

Lens · Technology architectureToday 1.5 / 52029 target 4.5 / 5

The platform bends at today's volume. It breaks at ten times.

My finding: the gateway is modern, but everything behind it is batch-era. I chose a strangler approach. I do not replace the core in one go. I put an event-driven hub in front of it and move instant payments across first.

One platform, one decision layer, one 24/7 desk.

What I take from this

Three things I learned building it.

  • Regulation sets the minimum. The operating model sets the margin.
  • The seams between teams cost more than any single system, so I designed around one decision layer and one desk.
  • A good roadmap is a series of decisions that stay reversible until the evidence arrives.
A
What I would do next with real data

I would mine real exception logs to replace my taxonomy shares, calibrate latency on production traces, and rebuild the liquidity curve from actual weekend flows.
